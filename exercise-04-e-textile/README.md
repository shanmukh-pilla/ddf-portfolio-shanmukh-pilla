# Exercise 04 – Blue Eye Mask (E-Textiles)

## Objective

The objective of this exercise was to explore E-Textiles by integrating electronic components into fabric using conductive thread. The task was to create a textile-based artifact containing sewable LEDs and a coin-cell battery holder while gaining practical experience with soft circuits, conductive materials, and wearable electronics.

---

## Process and Experimentation

### Planning the Design

I started by collecting all the required materials, including fabric, conductive thread, sewable LEDs, a coin-cell battery holder, scissors, and sewing tools. Before beginning the assembly process, I tested the LEDs and battery holder to ensure that all components were functioning correctly.

One of the first challenges was deciding what kind of textile artifact I wanted to create. Since this was my first experience with E-Textiles, I spent some time exploring different ideas before deciding to create a **Blue Eye Mask**. The intention was to create a simple wearable artifact that would allow me to focus on understanding the relationship between textiles and electronics.

I first created a sketch to plan the shape, LED placement, and battery holder position before transferring the design onto the fabric.

![Initial Sketch](images/sketch.jpg)

**Figure 1:** Initial sketch of the Blue Eye Mask showing the planned shape and placement of electronic components before fabrication.

Creating the sketch beforehand helped me visualize the conductive pathways and reduced the likelihood of mistakes during assembly.

---

### Constructing the Soft Circuit

After finalizing the design, I cut the fabric according to the planned shape and began attaching the LEDs and battery holder.

Initially, I made the mistake of using normal sewing thread for part of the assembly. After discussing my progress with the supervisor, I realized that the electrical connections needed to be stitched using conductive thread rather than regular thread. I then corrected the mistake and rebuilt the connections using conductive thread.

The LEDs and battery holder were stitched onto the fabric, and conductive thread was used to connect the positive and negative terminals throughout the circuit.

At this stage, I started understanding how soft circuits differ from traditional electronic circuits. Every stitch not only secured the component but also acted as an electrical pathway.

---

### Testing and Troubleshooting

Once the conductive stitching was completed, I inserted the battery and tested the circuit.

During the first few tests, some LEDs illuminated while others did not. After carefully inspecting the circuit and discussing the issue with the supervisor, I discovered that some of the conductive pathways were overlapping. The positive and negative conductive threads were touching each other in certain areas, creating unintended connections and preventing the LEDs from functioning correctly.

To solve this issue, I modified the routing of the conductive thread and ensured that the positive and negative pathways remained separated throughout the design.

After making these corrections, I tested the circuit again and all LEDs illuminated successfully.

![Working Circuit](images/working-circuit.jpg)

**Figure 2:** Completed soft circuit after correcting the conductive thread routing. All LEDs were successfully powered through the coin-cell battery holder.

The troubleshooting process was one of the most valuable parts of the exercise because it demonstrated how small mistakes in conductive stitching can directly affect the functionality of a soft circuit.

### Final Assembly

Once the circuit was fully operational, I added a second fabric layer over the electronic components. The purpose of this layer was to hide the conductive stitching and battery holder while maintaining the appearance of a wearable eye mask.

This step transformed the prototype from an exposed circuit into a more complete textile artifact.

## Observations

Throughout the exercise, I observed that working with conductive thread requires significantly more attention than working with conventional wires. Unlike traditional circuits, conductive stitching can easily create unintended connections if the routing is not carefully planned.

I also observed that testing frequently during construction is important because identifying mistakes becomes more difficult after multiple layers have been assembled.

Another interesting observation was how seamlessly electronics can be integrated into textiles. Before this exercise, I viewed fabric primarily as a structural or decorative material. This exercise demonstrated how textiles can also become functional electronic interfaces.

---

## Reflection

This exercise provided my first practical experience with E-Textiles and soft-circuit fabrication. Although the sewing process reminded me of crafting activities from school, integrating electronic functionality introduced an entirely new perspective.

The most challenging aspects were working with conductive thread, maintaining clean stitching, and ensuring that positive and negative pathways did not overlap. However, these challenges also became the most valuable learning opportunities because they required careful observation, testing, and iteration.

What I found most interesting was discovering how electronics can be embedded directly into textile materials without relying on rigid structures or traditional wiring. This exercise expanded my understanding of wearable technology and demonstrated how everyday materials can become interactive systems.

Overall, the Blue Eye Mask successfully introduced me to the fundamentals of E-Textiles and provided a strong foundation for exploring more advanced wearable and interactive textile applications in future projects.
